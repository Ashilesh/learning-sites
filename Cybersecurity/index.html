What is cybersecurity?

Imagine your computer is a house. Inside are your treasures: photos, games, messages, money. Cybersecurity is everything we do to keep bad guys out and keep treasures safe. Click the things on the house! 👇

=

Your house 🏠 ↔ Your computer 💻

🔐Door lock = Password
Only people with the key get in.
🚧Fence = Firewall
Decides who may even walk up to the house.
🪟Curtains = Encryption
People outside can't peek at what's inside.
🐕Guard dog = Antivirus
Sniffs out intruders that sneaked in.
📹Camera = Monitoring (like eBPF!)
Watches everything, raises an alarm.
🗄️Safe = Backups
Even if a thief gets in, your treasure has a copy.
📬Mailbox = Email
Watch out for tricky letters (phishing)!
🦹

Bad guys want…

to steal (passwords, money), spy (read your chats), or break things (lock your files, crash websites).

🦸

Good guys do…

Protect (locks & walls), Detect (cameras & alarms), Respond (kick them out), Recover (fix & restore).

🧠

You can help!

Most attacks start by tricking a person. A smart human is the best shield. That's you after this notebook! 😎

    Spot the Phish! 🎣

    Phishing = a fake message that tries to trick you into clicking a bad link or giving away secrets. This email is a trap! Find all 6 red flags by clicking on suspicious parts.

    From: MegaGame Support <support@megagame-prizes.xyz>
    Subject: ⚠️ URGENT!!! Your account will be DELETED in 1 hour

    Dear user,

    We noticed strange activity. Congratulation, you also won 10,000 free gems!!

    To keep your account, reply with your username and password or click below:

    Claim gems now 💎 → http://megagame.login-verify.ru/claim

    Thanks, The MegaGame Team

    Red flags found: 0/6 🚩

    1. Weird sender: real companies don't email from strange addresses like "-prizes.xyz".
    2. Panic & hurry: "URGENT! 1 hour!" They want you to act before you think.
    3. No name: real services usually know your name. "Dear user" is lazy.
    4. Too good to be true + spelling mistakes ("Congratulation").
    5. Asks for your password: NO real company EVER asks for it. Ever!
    6. Sneaky link: the button goes to a totally different website. Hover before you click!

    🎉 You're a phish-spotting pro! When in doubt, ask a grown-up or go to the app directly, never through the link.

    💬 The Sneaky Comment (XSS)
    
        
    A website shows everyone's comments. What if someone posts hidden code instead of words? If the site isn't careful, that code runs in every visitor's browser!
    
        
    
          
    maya_draws: love this drawing!! 🎨
    
          
    leo99: how did you do the shading?
    
        
    
        
    
        
    
          
          
        
    
        
    Try the sneaky comment with safe mode OFF, then ON.
    
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    🛡️ How builders stop this: they keep data and instructions apart (sealed envelopes), check every input, and follow a famous checklist of the most common website mistakes called the OWASP Top 10.
    
      
    ⚖️ Important rule: only try tricks like these on practice sites you own or have permission to test (like CTF games). Doing it to a real website without permission is against the law. Pros get permission first and report what they find.

    eBPF: Tiny Robot Guards in the Brain 🐝
    
    
    Every computer has a kernel: the boss of the computer that every app has to ask permission from ("Can I open this file?", "Can I talk to the internet?"). eBPF lets us put tiny, safe robot guards right inside the kernel, at the doors where those questions are asked, without rebuilding the kernel or restarting the computer. It's like adding smart cameras inside the walls of a building while everyone keeps working. 📹
    

    
    
      

      
    
        
    
          
        
    
        
    
          
        
    
        
    $ watching kernel events…
    # guards are OFF: nobody is watching! 🙈
    
        
    
          
        
    
      
    
    
    

    
    How a tiny eBPF guard gets hired 👷
    
    
    
      
    Write
    A programmer writes a tiny guard program: "if someone opens a secret file, tell me!"
    
      
    Verifier checks
    A strict inspector makes sure it can't crash the computer or loop forever. Unsafe? Rejected!
    
      
    Translate (JIT)
    It's turned into super-fast machine language so it barely slows anything down.
    
      
    Attach to a hook
    The guard sits at a door: running a program, opening files, network packets…
    
      
    Report & act
    It writes what it sees into a shared notebook (a "map"), or blocks bad actions right away.
    
    
    

    
    
      
    
        
    🌊 XDP: stopping a flood at the front door
    
        
    In a DDoS, millions of junk packets arrive. eBPF's XDP guard throws them out at the network card, before the kernel wastes energy on them.

    🧰 What people build with eBPF
    
        
      
          
    • 🚨 Intrusion alarms: tools like Falco & Tetragon shout when a container suddenly opens a shell or reads password files.
      • 
          
    • 🧱 Super-fast firewalls: Cilium and XDP filters drop bad traffic at millions of packets per second.
      • 
          
    • 🛑 Block bad actions: BPF-LSM can say "NO" to dangerous system calls in real time.
      • 
          
    • 🔭 See everything: who talked to whom, which program ran what, without changing the apps.
      • 
          
    • ⚠️ Careful! Because it's so powerful, only admins (root) may load eBPF programs, and attackers sometimes try to misuse it too. Guards must be guarded!

    📰 True-story style examples (simplified)
    
      
    
        
    💊 The hospital lock-out
    Ransomware once locked computers at many hospitals, so doctors had to go back to paper. Lesson: updates + backups would have saved them.
    
        
    🔑 The leaked password list
    Websites get hacked and millions of passwords leak. People who reused one password lost all accounts. Lesson: unique passwords + 2-step login.
    
        
    🎣 The fake boss email
    Companies have lost millions when someone pretended to be the boss and asked for an urgent money transfer. Lesson: double-check by phone.
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    🎩 Hacker Hats & Team Colours
    
      
      
    
        
    🔴 Red team
    pretends to be the attacker (with permission) to find weak spots
    
        
    🔵 Blue team
    defends: watches alarms, blocks attacks, fixes holes
    
        
    🟣 Purple team
    red + blue working together so everyone learns faster
    
      
    
      
    📜 The Good Hacker Code
    
      
    
        
    1. Permission first
    Get written OK from the owner before testing anything.
    
        
    2. Do no harm
    Don't break things or peek at data you don't need.
    
        
    3. Report it
    Tell the owner privately so they can fix it. Many pay "bug bounties"! 💰
    
        
    4. Practise legally
    CTF games, practice labs and your own computers are the playground.
