18Quantum Complexity Theory: What Quantum Computers Can and Cannot Do

⚡ In 30 seconds (no jargon, promise)

Computer scientists sort problems into 'classes' by how hard they are. P is easy for normal computers. NP is 'easy to check'. BQP is easy for quantum computers. Factoring sits in BQP (thanks to Shor), but the hardest NP problems, like the general travelling-salesman problem, almost certainly don't. Quantum computers can't compute anything uncomputable; they're just faster at some things.

18.1The complexity classes you need to know

ClassInformal meaning
PProblems a classical computer solves efficiently (polynomial time)
BPPProblems a classical computer with randomness solves efficiently with high probability
NPProblems whose solutions can be checked efficiently (e.g. sudoku, factoring)
NP-completeThe hardest problems in NP. If one is easy, all of NP is (e.g. SAT, travelling salesman decision version, graph colouring)
BQPProblems a quantum computer solves efficiently with high probability
QMAQuantum analogue of NP: solutions can be checked efficiently by a quantum computer given a quantum "proof"
PSPACEProblems solvable with polynomial memory (possibly exponential time)

Proven facts: P ⊆ BPP ⊆ BQP ⊆ PP ⊆ PSPACE. So a quantum computer can do anything a classical computer can, and anything a quantum computer can do, a classical computer can also do given enough time (with polynomial memory).

Believed but unproven:

  • BQP ≠ BPP: quantum computers really are more powerful. Proving it would also prove P ≠ PSPACE, a major open problem, so don't expect a proof soon.
  • NP-complete problems are not in BQP. Grover's optimality strongly suggests that quantum computers can't brute-force their way through them.
  • Factoring is not NP-complete. It sits in a special middle ground with lots of structure, which is exactly what Shor's algorithm exploits.

18.3Evidence that quantum computers are more powerful

  • Oracle separations: Simon's problem shows an exponential separation relative to an oracle. In 2018, Raz and Tal proved that relative to an oracle, BQP isn't even contained in the "polynomial hierarchy", a vast generalisation of NP.
  • Sampling hardness: if a classical computer could efficiently sample from the output distribution of random quantum circuits, boson sampling, or IQP circuits exactly, the polynomial hierarchy would collapse, which complexity theorists consider very unlikely. This is the theoretical basis of "quantum supremacy" experiments (Chapter 23).
  • Factoring: decades of effort have produced no efficient classical algorithm, while Shor's quantum algorithm is efficient.

18.4Things quantum computers can't do

  • Solve uncomputable problems. Anything a quantum computer can compute, a classical computer can also compute, just possibly much more slowly. Quantum computers don't break the Church–Turing thesis. They challenge only the extended (efficiency) version of it.
  • Efficiently find ground states of arbitrary systems. The local Hamiltonian problem is QMA-complete (Kitaev, 1999), so even quantum computers are believed unable to solve it in general. Quantum chemistry works because real molecules are not worst cases, and good starting states often exist.
  • Speed up everything. Many problems, like sorting, have provably no significant quantum speedup.

18.5Fun frontier results

  • MIP* = RE (2020): if you can interrogate two separated provers who share entanglement, you can verify solutions to problems as hard as the halting problem. A stunning result that also resolved long-standing questions in mathematics (Connes' embedding problem).
  • Classical verification of quantum computation (Mahadev, 2018): a purely classical user can, using cryptography, check that a quantum computer did its job honestly.
  • Quantum advantage in shallow circuits (Bravyi, Gosset, König, 2018): a provable, unconditional separation for constant-depth circuits.

⚖️ What complexity theory tells us

👍 The good
  • Strong evidence that quantum beats classical (oracle separations, sampling hardness)
  • A classical user can verify a quantum computation (Mahadev, 2018)
  • An unconditional advantage is proven for shallow circuits
👎 The tricky
  • BQP ≠ BPP is unproven (a proof would also show P ≠ PSPACE)
  • No efficient quantum algorithm expected for NP-complete problems
  • Ground states of arbitrary systems are QMA-complete

🎒 Pack these 3 ideas

  1. Proven: P ⊆ BPP ⊆ BQP ⊆ PP ⊆ PSPACE.
  2. Believed: NP-complete problems are not in BQP.
  3. Factoring isn't believed to be NP-complete; it has special structure.
