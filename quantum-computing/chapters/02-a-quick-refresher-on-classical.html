⚡ In 30 seconds (no jargon, promise)

Everything on your computer (photos, songs, this page) is a giant pile of 0s and 1s called bits. Logic gates (AND, OR, NOT...) shuffle them around. One sneaky detail matters a lot: most gates throw information away, but quantum physics never does. And classical probabilities can only add up. They can never cancel. Remember that, because it's the door into the quantum world.

To appreciate what's new, let's recall how ordinary ("classical") computers work.

2.1Bits

A bit is the smallest unit of information: 0 or 1. Physically, it might be a high or low voltage in a transistor, a magnetised spot on a disk, or a pit on a CD. Everything your computer does (photos, music, websites) is ultimately long strings of bits.

2.2Binary numbers

With n bits you can represent 2ⁿ different values, but only one at a time.

🧮 Pencil & paper

Worked example: The 4-bit string 1011 means 1×8 + 0×4 + 1×2 + 1×1 = 11. Four bits can represent any number from 0 (0000) to 15 (1111), which is 2⁴ = 16 values. But a 4-bit register holds exactly one of those 16 values at any moment.

Keep that last sentence in mind: a quantum register of n qubits can be in a superposition involving all 2ⁿ values simultaneously.

🤓 Did you know?

Erasing one bit must release at least kT ln 2 of heat: about 3×10⁻²¹ joules at room temperature (Landauer, 1961).

2.3Logic gates

Computers manipulate bits with logic gates:

The NAND gate alone is universal: any computation can be built from NAND gates. Quantum computing has its own universal gate sets (Chapter 6).

2.4Reversibility: the bridge to quantum

Look at the AND gate. If the output is 0, can you tell what the inputs were? No, because it could be 00, 01 or 10. AND destroys information, so it is irreversible.

Quantum physics, when left undisturbed, is always reversible: you can, in principle, run it backward. So every quantum gate must be reversible. Fortunately, any classical computation can be made reversible. The Toffoli gate (controlled-controlled-NOT) is a reversible gate that is universal for classical computation:

abc→abc ⊕ (a AND b)
000000
010010
100100
110111

(⊕ means XOR, i.e. addition mod 2.) Set c = 0 and the third output becomes a AND b, while the inputs are preserved. Consequence: a quantum computer can do anything a classical computer can do. It is a strict generalisation.

🔬 Deeper dive · optional

Deeper dive: Landauer's principle. Rolf Landauer showed in 1961 that erasing one bit of information must release at least kT ln 2 of heat (about 3×10⁻²¹ joules at room temperature). Reversible computing avoids erasure, which links information theory to thermodynamics. This idea, developed by Charles Bennett, helped inspire quantum computing.

2.5Algorithms and how hard problems are

Computer scientists measure difficulty by how the number of steps grows as the input size n grows:

Input size nn² (polynomial: "easy")2ⁿ (exponential: "hard")
101001,024
502,500~10¹⁵
10010,000~10³⁰
30090,000~10⁹⁰

Problems solvable in polynomial time are considered tractable. A big part of quantum computing is about problems that seem to need exponential time classically but polynomial time on a quantum computer.

2.6Randomness in classical computers

A classical random bit might be 0 with probability p₀ and 1 with probability p₁, where p₀ + p₁ = 1. Crucially, probabilities are never negative. Two ways of getting an outcome always add up to make it more likely. They can never cancel.

Quantum mechanics replaces probabilities with amplitudes, which can be negative (or even complex). Two paths to the same outcome can cancel each other out. This single difference, cancellation, is the root of quantum computing's power.

⚖️ Classical computers

👍 The good
  • Blazing fast, cheap and reliable: billions of operations per second
  • Perfect copies and backups are trivial
  • Great at almost everything we do every day
👎 The tricky
  • Exact simulation of quantum systems blows up exponentially
  • Irreversible gates erase information (and release heat, per Landauer)
  • Some problems, like factoring big numbers, seem to need exponential time

🎒 Pack these 3 ideas

  1. n bits can represent 2ⁿ values, but only one at a time.
  2. Quantum gates must be reversible. The Toffoli gate shows that classical logic can be reversible too.
  3. Probabilities never cancel. Quantum amplitudes can, and that is the whole game.

🧠 Check yourself +10 XP per first-try answer

1. What decimal number is the 4-bit string 1011?

1×8 + 0×4 + 1×2 + 1×1 = 11.

2. Why is the AND gate irreversible?

You can't work out the inputs from a 0 output.

3. What can quantum amplitudes do that probabilities can't?

Amplitudes can be negative or complex, so two paths can cancel. Probabilities only ever add.
