9No-Cloning, Teleportation and Superdense Coding

The hookTeleportation is real. It's been done from the ground to a satellite about 1,400 km away. Here's exactly how, with every amplitude shown.

⚡ In 30 seconds (no jargon, promise)

You can't photocopy an unknown quantum state; physics forbids it. But you can move one. If Alice and Bob share an entangled pair, Alice does a joint measurement, phones Bob two ordinary bits, and Bob fixes up his half so it becomes her original state. Nothing travels faster than light, and Alice's original is destroyed along the way. The mirror image, superdense coding, sends two bits by mailing a single qubit.

These protocols aren't "algorithms" that solve problems faster, but they reveal what quantum information can and cannot do, and they're the basis of quantum networks.

9.1The no-cloning theorem

Claim: No quantum operation can copy an arbitrary unknown state, i.e. turn |ψ⟩|0⟩ into |ψ⟩|ψ⟩ for every |ψ⟩.

🧮 Pencil & paper

Worked example showing why. The CNOT gate does copy basis states:

  • CNOT|0⟩|0⟩ = |0⟩|0⟩ ✓ (copied |0⟩)
  • CNOT|1⟩|0⟩ = |1⟩|1⟩ ✓ (copied |1⟩)

So try it on |+⟩:

  • What a true copier would give: |+⟩|+⟩ = (|00⟩ + |01⟩ + |10⟩ + |11⟩)/2
  • What CNOT actually gives: CNOT|+⟩|0⟩ = (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2, a Bell state!

They're different. Because quantum mechanics is linear, any machine that copies |0⟩ and |1⟩ must turn superpositions into entanglement rather than copies.

Short general proof: Suppose U clones two states, U|ψ⟩|0⟩ = |ψ⟩|ψ⟩ and U|φ⟩|0⟩ = |φ⟩|φ⟩. Unitaries preserve inner products, so ⟨φ|ψ⟩ = ⟨φ|ψ⟩². That's only possible if ⟨φ|ψ⟩ is 0 or 1, meaning the states are either identical or perfectly distinguishable. A general-purpose cloner is impossible. ∎

Consequences: no quantum "copy-paste" or backups (error correction must be cleverer, Chapter 20); eavesdroppers can't copy quantum keys (Chapter 25); and it's consistent with relativity (cloning would enable faster-than-light signalling).

9.2Quantum teleportation

Goal: Alice wants to send Bob an unknown qubit state |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩, without physically sending the qubit.

Resources: one shared Bell pair, plus two classical bits sent from Alice to Bob.

✏️ Show the original text sketch
q0 (Alice's |ψ⟩):  ─────────────●──H──M══════════╗
                                │                ║
q1 (Alice's half): ──H──●───────X─────M═══╗      ║
                        │                 ║      ║
q2 (Bob's half):   ─────X─────────────────X^m1───Z^m0── → |ψ⟩
                   └─Bell pair─┘   (apply X if m1=1, then Z if m0=1)
🧮 Pencil & paper

Full derivation. Start: |ψ⟩ ⊗ (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2, writing qubits in the order q0 q1 q2:

= [ α|000⟩ + α|011⟩ + β|100⟩ + β|111⟩ ] / √2

Step 1. Alice applies CNOT (q0 → q1): flip q1 whenever q0 = 1.

= [ α|000⟩ + α|011⟩ + β|110⟩ + β|101⟩ ] / √2

Step 2. Alice applies H to q0 and we regroup by Alice's two qubits:

= ½ [ |00⟩(α|0⟩ + β|1⟩)
    + |01⟩(α|1⟩ + β|0⟩)
    + |10⟩(α|0⟩ − β|1⟩)
    + |11⟩(α|1⟩ − β|0⟩) ]

Step 3. Alice measures q0 and q1 and phones Bob the two bits. Each of the four outcomes is equally likely (25%), and Bob's qubit is left in a state closely related to |ψ⟩:

Alice measures (m0 m1)Bob's qubit isBob appliesBob gets
00α|0⟩ + β|1⟩nothing|ψ⟩ ✓
01α|1⟩ + β|0⟩X|ψ⟩ ✓
10α|0⟩ − β|1⟩Z|ψ⟩ ✓
11α|1⟩ − β|0⟩X, then Z|ψ⟩ ✓

Key points:

  • Nothing travels faster than light. Until Bob receives the two classical bits, his qubit looks completely random.
  • No cloning happens. Alice's original is destroyed by her measurement.
  • Nobody ever learns α and β. The state is transferred without being read.
  • It's been demonstrated many times: in 1997 (Innsbruck and Rome), across 143 km between two Canary Islands (2012), and from the ground to the Micius satellite over distances up to about 1,400 km (2017).
  • Gate teleportation, a variant, is a core trick in fault-tolerant computing and in linking separate quantum processors.

9.3Superdense coding

Teleportation sends 1 qubit using 2 classical bits plus entanglement. Superdense coding is its mirror image: it sends 2 classical bits by transmitting 1 qubit, plus pre-shared entanglement.

  1. Alice and Bob share |Φ⁺⟩ = (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2. Alice holds q0 and Bob holds q1.
  2. Alice encodes two bits by applying a gate to her qubit only, then sends it to Bob.
  3. Bob applies CNOT (q0 → q1), then H on q0, and measures both.
MessageAlice appliesShared state becomesAfter Bob's CNOT + HBob reads
00I(|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2|00⟩00 ✓
01X(|10⟩ + |01⟩)/√2|01⟩01 ✓
10Z(|00⟩ − |11⟩)/√2|10⟩10 ✓
11X then Z(|01⟩ − |10⟩)/√2|11⟩11 ✓

This doesn't violate Holevo's bound: Bob uses two qubits in the decoding (one of which was delivered in advance).

9.4Entanglement swapping

If Alice-and-Charlie share a Bell pair and Charlie-and-Bob share another, Charlie can do a joint "Bell measurement" on his two qubits. The result: Alice's and Bob's qubits become entangled, even though they never interacted. This is how quantum repeaters will extend entanglement over long distances (Chapter 26).

9.5The CHSH game: proving entanglement beats anything classical

This cooperative game makes Bell's theorem concrete.

  • A referee sends Alice a random bit x and Bob a random bit y. They can't communicate.
  • Alice answers with bit a, Bob with bit b.
  • They win if a ⊕ b = x AND y. In words: their answers should match, unless both received 1, in which case their answers should differ.
StrategyBest possible win rate
Any classical strategy (even with shared random numbers)75% (e.g. always answer 0: you win in 3 of the 4 cases)
Sharing a Bell pair and measuring at clever anglescos²(π/8) ≈ 85.4%

No classical "pre-agreed plan" (no hidden variable) can exceed 75%. Quantum entanglement reaches 85.4%, and experiments confirm it. The limit of 85.4% (the Tsirelson bound) shows that even quantum mechanics has limits: it doesn't allow arbitrary correlations. In 2015, three "loophole-free" Bell tests (Delft, Vienna and NIST Boulder) closed the last major experimental loopholes.

This game is also the basis of device-independent cryptography and certified randomness: if devices win the game more than 75% of the time, they must be quantum, even if you don't trust whoever built them.

9.6A few more quantum information concepts

ConceptPlain meaning
FidelityHow close two quantum states are (1 = identical, 0 = perfectly distinguishable). Used to report gate quality, e.g. "99.9% two-qubit gate fidelity"
Von Neumann entropyQuantum version of uncertainty or information content. For one half of a Bell pair it's 1 bit: maximal
Entanglement entropyHow entangled one part of a system is with the rest. Low entanglement means easier classical simulation
Quantum channelAny physically allowed process, including noise. Described by "Kraus operators"
Holevo boundn qubits can carry at most n classical bits
No-signallingLocal operations on one half of an entangled system can't send information to the other half

⚖️ Quantum information protocols

👍 The good
  • Move a qubit's state without moving the particle
  • Eavesdroppers can't copy secrets
  • Bell tests certify 'quantumness', even for untrusted devices
👎 The tricky
  • Teleportation still needs a classical channel, so no faster-than-light messages
  • Needs high-quality entanglement shared in advance
  • No-cloning makes backups and error correction harder

🎒 Pack these 3 ideas

  1. No-cloning: linearity forbids copying arbitrary unknown states.
  2. Teleportation = 1 Bell pair + 2 classical bits → 1 qubit moved.
  3. CHSH game: classical best is 75%, quantum reaches ≈ 85.4% (the Tsirelson bound).
