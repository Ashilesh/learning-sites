27.1Turning fragility into a feature

Quantum states are exquisitely sensitive to their environment. That's a curse for computing but a gift for measurement. A qubit that dephases because of a tiny magnetic field is, by the same token, an extraordinarily good magnetic-field detector.

27.3The standard quantum limit and the Heisenberg limit

With N independent particles, measurement precision improves as 1/√N. That's the standard quantum limit. With entanglement, it can improve as 1/N, the Heisenberg limit.

🧮 Pencil & paper

Example: with N = 1,000,000 atoms, independent atoms give precision ∝ 1/1,000, while ideally entangled atoms give ∝ 1/1,000,000, a thousand times better. In practice, entangled states are fragile, so real gains are smaller, but squeezing is already used in clocks and LIGO.

27.4Status

Quantum sensing is arguably the most commercially mature quantum technology after atomic clocks and QRNGs. Quantum navigation systems that work without GPS have been trialled on ships, aircraft and trains, which is valuable as GPS jamming grows more common.

⚖️ Quantum sensing

👍 The good
  • Already commercial: atomic clocks, MRI, SQUIDs, magnetometers
  • GPS-free navigation as jamming grows more common
  • LIGO uses squeezed light to observe more events
👎 The tricky
  • Entangled states are fragile, so real gains are smaller than ideal
  • Many devices need lasers, vacuum or cryogenics
  • Moving from the lab to the field is hard engineering

🎒 Pack these 3 ideas

  1. Fragility becomes a feature.
  2. Standard quantum limit 1/√N versus Heisenberg limit 1/N.
  3. Arguably the most commercially mature quantum technology today.
