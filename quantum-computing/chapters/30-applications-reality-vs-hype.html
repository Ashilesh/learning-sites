30Applications: Reality vs. Hype

The hookQuantum will revolutionise everything... right? Let's grade every industry from 'real' to 'very high hype'.

⚡ In 30 seconds (no jargon, promise)

Quantum computers will be specialists. The best problems have a small input, a small output, and an enormous tangle in between that classical computers can't handle, like a molecule's energy or the factors of a number. Chemistry and fundamental science look most promising; logistics and AI claims are the most hyped. And no, it won't make your games faster.

30.2Things quantum computers will probably not do

  • Speed up web browsing, video, gaming or office software.
  • Replace GPUs for training large AI models.
  • Solve NP-hard optimisation problems exactly and efficiently.
  • Analyse huge classical datasets faster (loading data is the bottleneck).
  • Run "everything in parallel".
🤓 Did you know?

The Haber–Bosch fertiliser process uses roughly 1–2% of the world's energy, which makes it a prime target for quantum chemistry.

30.3The "Goldilocks" profile of a good quantum problem

The best candidates for quantum advantage have:

  1. Small input: a molecule's description, a number to factor.
  2. Small output: an energy value, a pair of factors.
  3. Enormous internal complexity that is exponentially hard for classical computers.
  4. Structure that quantum interference can exploit.

30.4A rough timeline (highly uncertain)

~2027–2030Early fault-tolerant machines with tens to hundreds of logical qubits; first scientifically valuable simulations that beat classical methods

⚠️ Heads up

Treat any precise prediction, including this one, with caution. Breakthroughs and setbacks both happen.

⚖️ Quantum applications

👍 The good
  • Chemistry and materials: the strongest case
  • Fundamental science: likely the first genuine advantage
  • Sensing and navigation: already commercial
👎 The tricky
  • Logistics and optimisation: no proven advantage, very high hype
  • AI and machine learning: uncertain, very high hype
  • Finance: quadratic speedups may need very large machines

🎒 Pack these 3 ideas

  1. The Goldilocks problem: small input, small output, huge internal complexity, exploitable structure.
  2. Lowest hype: fundamental science, chemistry and materials, sensing.
  3. Quantum won't speed up browsing, gaming or AI training.
