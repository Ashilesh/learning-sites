⚡ In 30 seconds (no jargon, promise)

'Quantum supremacy' means a quantum computer did something (anything, even something useless) that no classical computer realistically could. 'Quantum advantage' usually means doing something useful better. Every big claim sparks a race by classical programmers to match it, and sometimes they do. Learn seven questions and you'll never be fooled by a headline again.

23.1Words that get confused

TermMeaning
Quantum supremacy (Preskill, 2012)A quantum computer performs some task, even a useless one, that's infeasible for any classical computer
Beyond-classicalA more neutral synonym preferred by many researchers
Quantum advantageUsually means a quantum computer beats classical methods on a useful or practical task. Usage varies, so check what's claimed
Quantum utility (IBM's term)Quantum computers producing reliable results at a scale beyond brute-force classical simulation, useful as scientific tools
Fault toleranceComputation protected by error correction, so it can run long and reliably

23.4The moving target

Every beyond-classical claim triggers a race by classical algorithm designers. Tensor networks, Pauli-path methods, belief propagation and clever use of GPUs have repeatedly narrowed claimed gaps. This is healthy science: it sharpens what "advantage" really means. The trend, however, is that newer experiments, especially those with error correction or verifiable outputs, are much harder to match classically.

23.5How to read a quantum computing headline

Ask these questions:

  1. Is the task useful, or is it a benchmark designed to be hard?
  2. Compared to what? The best classical algorithm on the best hardware, or a naive method?
  3. Peer-reviewed, or a press release or preprint?
  4. Physical or logical qubits? And what are the error rates?
  5. Does it scale, or is it a one-off trick?
  6. Who's making the claim, and do they sell the hardware?
  7. Has anyone independent reproduced or challenged it?

⚖️ Quantum benchmarks

👍 The good
  • Random circuit sampling is now far beyond classical (Willow: an estimated 10²⁵ years on Frontier)
  • Verifiable outputs are making claims much stronger
  • Healthy competition sharpens what 'advantage' means
👎 The tricky
  • Qubit counts alone are meaningless
  • Many claims were later matched classically
  • Hype and marketing blur the vocabulary

🎒 Pack these 3 ideas

  1. Supremacy (beyond-classical) is not the same as useful advantage.
  2. Claims are a moving target: classical methods keep catching up.
  3. Ask: useful? compared to what? peer-reviewed? physical or logical qubits? reproduced?
