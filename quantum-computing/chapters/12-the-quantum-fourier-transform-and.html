A Fourier transform finds the repeating rhythms hidden in a signal, the way your ear picks out the notes of a chord. The quantum Fourier transform does this to the amplitudes of a quantum state. If the state repeats with some period, the output spikes at values that reveal that period. Phase estimation uses the same trick to read off a hidden angle, and it's the master key for Shor, chemistry and more.

12.1Fourier transforms, intuitively

A Fourier transform breaks a signal into its frequencies.

💡 Lightbulb moment

Analogy: your ear does a Fourier transform. When you hear a piano chord, you perceive the individual notes, even though only one combined pressure wave reaches your ear. The Fourier transform "un-mixes" a signal into its repeating components.

If a sequence repeats with some period r, its Fourier transform has sharp peaks that reveal r. Finding hidden periods is the heart of Shor's algorithm.

12.2The Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT)

For N = 2ⁿ basis states, the QFT maps:

|x⟩  →  (1/√N) Σₖ e^(2πi·x·k/N) |k⟩
  • For 1 qubit (N = 2), the QFT is just the Hadamard gate.
  • For 2 qubits (N = 4):
QFT₄ = ½ · [1   1   1   1]
           [1   i  −1  −i]
           [1  −1   1  −1]
           [1  −i  −1   i]
🧮 Pencil & paper

Worked example: the QFT finds periods. Take a state with period 2 inside N = 4, offset by 1: (|1⟩ + |3⟩)/√2.

  • QFT|1⟩ = ½(|0⟩ + i|1⟩ − |2⟩ − i|3⟩)
  • QFT|3⟩ = ½(|0⟩ − i|1⟩ − |2⟩ + i|3⟩)
  • Sum ÷ √2 = (|0⟩ − |2⟩)/√2

The |1⟩ and |3⟩ terms cancelled out. The only possible outcomes are 0 and 2, multiples of N/r = 4/2 = 2. Try the offset-0 version (|0⟩ + |2⟩)/√2 and you'll also get peaks at 0 and 2. The offset only changes the phases, never where the peaks are. So measuring tells us about the period regardless of the offset, which is exactly what Shor needs.

12.3The QFT is exponentially efficient

Operations for 2ⁿ numbers
Classical Fast Fourier TransformAbout n · 2ⁿ
Quantum Fourier TransformAbout n² gates (Hadamards and controlled phase rotations)

For n = 50, that's about 5.6 × 10¹⁶ versus 2,500. Catch: the QFT doesn't hand you all 2ⁿ Fourier coefficients. You get to sample one outcome. It's only useful when sampling reveals what you need, like a period.

A 3-qubit QFT circuit:

✏️ Show the original text sketch
q0: ──H──R2──R3──────────────×──
         │   │               │
q1: ─────●───┼───H──R2───────┼──
             │      │        │
q2: ─────────●──────●───H────×──

R_k = phase rotation by 2π/2^k, controlled by the qubit below.  × ─ × = final SWAP to reverse bit order

12.4Quantum Phase Estimation (QPE)

Problem: You have a unitary U and one of its eigenstates |u⟩, with U|u⟩ = e^(2πiθ)|u⟩. Estimate the phase θ (a number between 0 and 1).

How:

  1. Prepare t "counting" qubits in superposition (H gates) and the eigenstate |u⟩ in a second register.
  2. Apply controlled-U, controlled-U², controlled-U⁴, …, controlled-U^(2^(t−1)). Phase kickback writes θ into the counting register as a "frequency".
  3. Apply the inverse QFT to the counting register.
  4. Measure to get the binary digits of θ.
✏️ Show the original text sketch
counting q0: |0⟩─H───────────────────────●─────┤          ├─M
counting q1: |0⟩─H───────────────●───────┼─────┤ QFT⁻¹    ├─M
counting q2: |0⟩─H───────●───────┼───────┼─────┤          ├─M
                         │       │       │
eigenstate:  |u⟩────────[U]────[U²]────[U⁴]────────────────
🧮 Pencil & paper

Worked example: estimate the phase of the T gate.
T|1⟩ = e^(iπ/4)|1⟩ = e^(2πi · 1/8)|1⟩, so θ = 1/8. In binary, 1/8 = 0.001.
With 3 counting qubits, QPE outputs 001 with 100% probability. If θ doesn't have an exact t-bit binary expansion, QPE returns a close approximation with high probability, and adding a few extra qubits improves both precision and reliability.

12.5Why QPE is everywhere

ApplicationWhat the "phase" encodes
Shor's algorithmThe period r (via the eigenphases of modular multiplication)
Quantum chemistryEnergy levels of a molecule (eigenvalues of e^(−iHt))
HHL linear solverEigenvalues of a matrix, which get inverted
Quantum countingThe number of solutions to a search problem

⚖️ QFT and phase estimation

👍 The good
  • Exponentially efficient
  • One subroutine powers Shor, chemistry, HHL and counting
  • The peaks don't depend on the unknown offset
👎 The tricky
  • You only get to sample one outcome, not all 2ⁿ coefficients
  • QPE needs deep controlled-U^(2^k) circuits
  • Needs a good eigenstate (or something close) to start from

🎒 Pack these 3 ideas

  1. The QFT needs about n² gates, versus about n·2ⁿ operations for the classical FFT.
  2. A periodic state gives sharp peaks at multiples of N/r, whatever the offset.
  3. QPE estimates eigenphases and is used in Shor, chemistry, HHL and quantum counting.
