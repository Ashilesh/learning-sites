⚡ In 30 seconds (no jargon, promise)

A qubit is like an arrow that can point anywhere on a globe. North pole = 0, south pole = 1, anywhere else = a blend. The closer it points to the north, the more likely you are to read 0. Every point on the equator gives 50/50, but they differ in a hidden 'phase' that quantum algorithms use as a steering wheel. When you read the qubit you get just one bit, and the arrow snaps to a pole.

4.1From bit to qubit

A qubit has two basis states, written |0⟩ and |1⟩. These play the role of 0 and 1. But a qubit can also be in a superposition:

|ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩
  • α and β are amplitudes: numbers that can be positive, negative or complex.
  • If you measure, you get 0 with probability |α|² and 1 with probability |β|².
  • The probabilities must add to 1: |α|² + |β|² = 1. This is called normalisation.

4.2Amplitudes vs. probabilities

🧮 Pencil & paper

Worked example 1: the equal superposition.
|+⟩ = (1/√2)|0⟩ + (1/√2)|1⟩

  • P(0) = (1/√2)² = 1/2 = 50%
  • P(1) = (1/√2)² = 1/2 = 50%
🧮 Pencil & paper

Worked example 2: an unequal superposition.
|ψ⟩ = (√3/2)|0⟩ + (1/2)|1⟩

  • P(0) = (√3/2)² = 3/4 = 75%
  • P(1) = (1/2)² = 1/4 = 25%
  • Check: 3/4 + 1/4 = 1 ✓
🧮 Pencil & paper

Worked example 3: a negative amplitude.
|−⟩ = (1/√2)|0⟩ − (1/√2)|1⟩

  • P(0) = 50%, P(1) = 50%, which is exactly the same as |+⟩!

So are |+⟩ and |−⟩ the same? No. The minus sign is invisible to a single measurement, but it completely changes how the state interferes. Apply a Hadamard gate (Chapter 6) and |+⟩ becomes |0⟩ with certainty while |−⟩ becomes |1⟩ with certainty. That hidden sign is the "steering wheel" quantum algorithms use.

🧮 Pencil & paper

Worked example 4: a complex amplitude.
|ψ⟩ = (1/√2)|0⟩ + (i/√2)|1⟩, where i = √−1.

  • P(1) = |i/√2|² = |i|²/2 = 1/2. (For complex numbers, |a + bi|² = a² + b², and |i|² = 1.)
🤓 Did you know?

A qubit can be a superconducting circuit, a single ion, a neutral atom, a photon, an electron spin in silicon, or a defect in diamond.

4.3Phase

Amplitudes have a size and a "direction" (an angle, called the phase). Two kinds:

  • Global phase: multiplying the whole state by the same factor (e.g. −|ψ⟩) has no observable effect whatsoever. |ψ⟩ and −|ψ⟩ are physically the same state.
  • Relative phase: the phase between |0⟩ and |1⟩ (like the minus sign in |−⟩) is physically real and measurable through interference.

4.4The Bloch sphere: a picture of a qubit

Any single-qubit state (ignoring global phase) can be written as:

|ψ⟩ = cos(θ/2)|0⟩ + e^(iφ) sin(θ/2)|1⟩

The two angles θ (0 to 180°) and φ (0 to 360°) are like latitude and longitude, so every qubit state is a point on the surface of a sphere, the Bloch sphere:

⚠️ Heads up

The Bloch sphere only works for one qubit. There's no simple 3-D picture for entangled multi-qubit states, which is part of why they're so powerful.

4.5Measuring in different bases

"Measuring a qubit" normally means asking "are you |0⟩ or |1⟩?" This is the Z basis (or computational basis). But you can ask other questions, like "are you |+⟩ or |−⟩?" That's the X basis.

🧮 Pencil & paper

Worked example:

  • Qubit in |+⟩, measured in the Z basis → 50% 0, 50% 1. Completely random.
  • The same qubit measured in the X basis → + with 100% certainty.

A state that is perfectly certain in one basis is completely uncertain in the other. This is complementarity (Rule 6) in action, and it's the foundation of the BB84 quantum key distribution protocol.

In practice, hardware usually only measures in the Z basis. To measure in the X basis, you apply a Hadamard gate first, then measure in Z.

4.6How much information does a qubit hold?

Describing a qubit requires two real numbers (θ, φ) with infinite precision, which seems like infinite information! But Holevo's theorem (1973) proves you can extract at most one classical bit per qubit by measurement. A qubit's richness is only accessible indirectly, through interference during a computation.

🔬 Deeper dive · optional

Deeper dive: With entanglement, you can send 2 classical bits by transmitting 1 qubit, if the receiver already holds the other half of an entangled pair. This is superdense coding (Chapter 9).

4.8Pure states, mixed states and density matrices (optional)

Everything so far describes pure states, states we know exactly. Real qubits are often in mixed states, uncertain mixtures caused by noise. These are described by a density matrix ρ.

🧮 Pencil & paper

Worked example: superposition vs. a coin flip.

  • State A: |+⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2
  • State B: "We flipped a coin: it's |0⟩ or |1⟩, 50/50, but we don't know which."

Measured in the Z basis, both give 50/50. Are they the same? No! Apply a Hadamard and measure:

  • State A → always 0 (the superposition interferes perfectly).
  • State B → still 50/50 (there's nothing to interfere; it was just ignorance).

On the Bloch sphere, pure states live on the surface and mixed states live inside (the "Bloch ball"). The maximally mixed state, total ignorance, is at the very centre. Decoherence pushes states from the surface toward the centre.

⚖️ Qubits vs. bits

👍 The good
  • Holds a continuum of states: any point on the Bloch sphere
  • Phase is a hidden 'steering wheel' for interference
  • Many physical options: circuits, ions, atoms, photons, spins
👎 The tricky
  • Reading one gives just 1 bit (Holevo's theorem)
  • Measuring collapses the state
  • Fragile: noise drags states into 'mixed' states inside the sphere

🎒 Pack these 3 ideas

  1. |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩, with probabilities |α|² and |β|² that add up to 1.
  2. Relative phase (like the minus sign in |−⟩) is invisible to a single measurement but changes interference.
  3. Holevo's theorem: you can read out at most one classical bit per qubit.

🧠 Check yourself +10 XP per first-try answer

1. A qubit is in (√3/2)|0⟩ + (1/2)|1⟩. What is P(0)?

(√3/2)² = 3/4 = 75%.

2. |+⟩ and |−⟩ both give 50/50 in the Z basis. Are they the same state?

A Hadamard turns |+⟩ into |0⟩ and |−⟩ into |1⟩, both with certainty.

3. Where do mixed (noisy) states live on the Bloch sphere?

Pure states sit on the surface. Mixed states sit inside, with total ignorance at the centre.
