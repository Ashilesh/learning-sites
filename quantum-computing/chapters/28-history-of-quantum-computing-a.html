28History of Quantum Computing: A Timeline

⏱ 4 min7 sections🟢 Curious reader🟡 Student✅ Completed

The hook126 years, several Nobel Prizes and one famous cat. The story of how we got here.

⚡ In 30 seconds (no jargon, promise)

Quantum computing grew out of a 1900 puzzle about light, a 1935 argument between Einstein and Bohr, a 1981 lecture by Feynman, and a 1994 algorithm that put every intelligence agency on alert. Since then it has gone from 2-qubit lab demos to cloud machines, supremacy claims and, in 2024–2026, working error correction.

28.1Building quantum mechanics (1900-1935)

  1. 1900
    Max Planck proposes that energy is emitted in discrete quanta
  2. 1905
    Albert Einstein explains the photoelectric effect with light quanta (photons)
  3. 1913
    Niels Bohr's model of the atom with discrete energy levels
  4. 1924
    Louis de Broglie proposes that matter behaves like waves
  5. 1925
    Werner Heisenberg develops matrix mechanics
  6. 1926
    Erwin Schrödinger's wave equation; Max Born's probability rule
  7. 1927
    Heisenberg's uncertainty principle; the Bohr–Einstein debates at the Solvay Conference
  8. 1932
    John von Neumann puts quantum mechanics on rigorous mathematical foundations (Hilbert spaces)
  9. 1935
    Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen (EPR) argue quantum mechanics is incomplete; Schrödinger coins "entanglement" and describes his famous cat

28.2Information meets physics (1948-1980)

  1. 1948
    Claude Shannon founds information theory
  2. 1957
    Hugh Everett proposes the many-worlds interpretation
  3. 1961
    Rolf Landauer: erasing information costs energy
  4. 1964
    John Bell proves his theorem: no local hidden-variable theory can reproduce quantum predictions
  5. ~1970
    Stephen Wiesner invents "conjugate coding" and quantum money (published 1983)
  6. 1972
    Freedman and Clauser perform the first Bell test
  7. 1973
    Alexander Holevo's bound; Charles Bennett shows computation can be reversible
  8. 1980
    Paul Benioff describes a quantum-mechanical Turing machine; Yuri Manin suggests quantum computation
🤓 Did you know?

Schrödinger coined the word 'entanglement' in 1935, the same year he described his famous cat.

28.3The birth of quantum computing (1981-1994)

  1. 1981
    Richard Feynman's lecture "Simulating Physics with Computers" (published 1982)
  2. 1982
    Alain Aspect's Bell-test experiments; the no-cloning theorem (Wootters & Zurek; Dieks)
  3. 1984
    Bennett and Brassard: BB84 quantum key distribution
  4. 1985
    David Deutsch defines the universal quantum computer and the first quantum algorithm
  5. 1991
    Artur Ekert: entanglement-based QKD (E91)
  6. 1992
    Deutsch–Jozsa algorithm
  7. 1993
    Bernstein–Vazirani algorithm; quantum teleportation proposed (Bennett et al.)
  8. 1994
    Simon's algorithm; Peter Shor's factoring algorithm

28.4First experiments (1995-2010)

  1. 1995
    Shor's 9-qubit error-correcting code; Cirac and Zoller propose ion-trap quantum computing; Monroe and Wineland (NIST) demonstrate the first quantum logic gate
  2. 1996
    Lov Grover's search algorithm; Seth Lloyd proves universal quantum simulation; Steane code
  3. 1996–97
    The threshold theorem; Gottesman's stabilizer formalism; Kitaev's toric code
  4. 1997
    First teleportation experiments (Innsbruck and Rome)
  5. 1998
    First quantum algorithms run on 2-qubit NMR machines
  6. 1999
    Nakamura, Pashkin and Tsai demonstrate a superconducting qubit
  7. 2000
    DiVincenzo's criteria; Nielsen and Chuang's textbook, still the field's "bible"
  8. 2001
    IBM factors 15 with a 7-qubit NMR device; KLM scheme for linear-optical quantum computing; measurement-based quantum computing (Raussendorf & Briegel)
  9. 2004
    First QKD-secured bank transfer (Vienna)
  10. 2007
    The transmon qubit (Yale), now the most widely used superconducting qubit
  11. 2009
    HHL linear-systems algorithm

28.5The industry era begins (2011-2018)

  1. 2011
    D-Wave One, the first commercial quantum annealer; boson sampling proposed
  2. 2012
    Nobel Prize in Physics to David Wineland and Serge Haroche for controlling individual quantum systems; Preskill coins "quantum supremacy"
  3. 2014
    VQE and QAOA proposed; Google hires John Martinis's group to build superconducting processors
  4. 2015
    Three loophole-free Bell tests (Delft, Vienna, NIST)
  5. 2016
    IBM puts a 5-qubit quantum computer on the cloud for anyone to use; China launches the Micius satellite
  6. 2017
    IBM releases Qiskit; Micius demonstrates satellite entanglement distribution and teleportation
  7. 2018
    Preskill coins "NISQ"; the US National Quantum Initiative Act

28.6Supremacy and scaling (2019-2023)

  1. 2019
    Google's Sycamore (53 qubits) claims quantum supremacy; IBM Q System One, the first integrated commercial system
  2. 2020
    USTC's Jiuzhang photonic boson-sampling experiment
  3. 2021
    IBM Eagle (127 qubits); USTC Zuchongzhi superconducting supremacy experiments
  4. 2022
    Nobel Prize in Physics to Alain Aspect, John Clauser and Anton Zeilinger for entanglement experiments; Xanadu Borealis; IBM Osprey (433 qubits); SIKE broken classically
  5. 2023
    IBM Condor (1,121 qubits) and Heron; IBM "quantum utility" paper; Google shows larger surface codes outperform smaller ones; Harvard/MIT/QuEra run 48 logical qubits

28.7The error-correction era (2024-2026)

  1. 2024
    NIST publishes the first post-quantum standards (FIPS 203, 204, 205); Microsoft and Quantinuum demonstrate high-fidelity logical qubits; Microsoft and Atom Computing entangle 24 logical qubits; IBM's gross-code paper; Google DeepMind's AlphaQubit decoder; Google Willow shows below-threshold error correction
  2. 2025
    International Year of Quantum Science and Technology (UN), marking 100 years of quantum mechanics; Microsoft's Majorana 1 announcement (contested); AWS Ocelot; Gidney estimates RSA-2048 could fall to under 1 million noisy qubits; Caltech traps 6,100 atoms; JPMorgan Chase and Quantinuum demonstrate certified randomness; NIST selects HQC; Nobel Prize in Physics to John Clarke, Michel Devoret and John Martinis for discovering macroscopic quantum tunnelling and energy quantisation in an electrical circuit, the foundation of superconducting qubits; Google's Quantum Echoes verifiable-advantage experiment; IBM Nighthawk and Loon; Quantinuum Helios (98 qubits); IonQ acquires Oxford Ionics
  3. 2026
    IBM and the University of Chicago report a beyond-classical sampling experiment on 70 logical qubits (preprint, July); Quantinuum reports 99.9995% logical fidelity on Helios (October) and targets its Sol system for 2027 and Apollo, a universal fault-tolerant machine, by 2029

⚖️ How far we've come

👍 Big wins
  • Error correction working below threshold (2024)
  • Post-quantum standards published (2024)
  • Cloud quantum computers open to everyone since 2016
👎 Still waiting for
  • A commercially useful quantum advantage
  • A cryptographically relevant quantum computer
  • Independently confirmed topological qubits

🎒 Pack these 3 ideas

  1. 1900–1935: quantum mechanics is built.
  2. 1981–1994: Feynman, Deutsch, BB84 and Shor. The field is born.
  3. 2024–2026: the error-correction era.
